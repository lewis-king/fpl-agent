## Shared-world stateful plans

`weekly_policy_research.py` constructs up to four ordinary first-action roots, including the
published action and roll, plus one root per available chip. Each root carries its actual next
manager state: holdings, purchase/selling prices, cash, FTs, chip consumption and restoration.

At the next deadline it solves 0–2 transfers, at most one paid transfer, and available chip
alternatives. It retains one screening winner for each remaining chip-inventory class. It never
collapses different remaining chip sets into a supposedly comparable free scalar value.

The resulting two-deadline plans are scored in the **same already-generated production worlds**.
No future sampled score is visible when constructing a plan. Current and future hits are charged
once at the correct discount; terminal FT value appears once. The no-chip continuation comparison
uses the production selector and a roll-now/allow-moves-next-week comparator.

The rehearsal completed eight roots and **36 branches** in 7.9 seconds of plan construction.
Its ordinary continuation preferred rolling now, whereas the operational fixed-plan selector
preferred Richarlison → Evanilson. This disagreement is preserved, not hidden. These are still
origin-forecast, flat-price, open-loop projections; they are not a calibrated distribution of
future injury news or a fully contingent season policy. The future paid-hit policy and terminal
chip value still require matched sequential validation. Consequently `decision_authority` is false.

Joint chip branches report raw projected upside and exact future teams, but no operational chip
recommendation. Unpriced retained Wildcard/Free Hit opportunities are not treated as worthless.
The research diagnostic cannot block an otherwise valid ordinary recommendation.

## Complete historical chip-state audit

`joint_chip_state_replay.py` uses 2024/25 and 2025/26 player forecasts/outcomes and point-in-time
fixture/catalog archives. A **precommitted exercise calendar**, not an optimized timing policy,
plays WC5→BB6, TC10, FH18, WC24→BB25, TC30 and FH37. Ordinary legal moves run between those dates.
This tests both chip halves, FT carryover, sale proceeds, permanent/temporary squads and scoring.

The first attempt correctly stopped on two problems:

- The older OOF forecast table omitted held Liverpool players in blank GW15 of 2024/25.
- A real-club transfer in 2025/26 created a held four-from-one-club squad, which our state validator
  incorrectly rejected before it could consider either holding or a legal repairing transfer.

The fixes are deliberately narrow:

1. `historical_blank_holdings.py` reconstructs missing **held** player projections from an earlier
   own-model neutral rate and the origin's known fixture schedule/catalog. It labels these as
   reconstructed forecasts, not original OOF observations. A zero actual score requires a separate,
   complete 380-match final-season certificate proving that the club had no fixture in that event.
   Future final fixtures cannot change the forecast values or prices. Unknown outcomes still fail.
2. `club_overflow_allowed` is an explicit held-state provenance flag. It is set from an authenticated
   existing squad or a witnessed real-club transition. A roll can keep it; any actual fantasy
   transfer must restore the ordinary club limit. A Free Hit restores the original flag/holdings;
   a permanent repair clears it. New squad construction remains capped at three.

The latter matches the [official FPL explanation](https://www.premierleague.com/en/news/4536241):
an existing over-limit squad resulting from a real transfer must be repaired when next making
fantasy transfers. It does not authorize deliberately building an illegal new squad.

Both full paths now complete: **76 events and 16 chip uses**. This is mechanical validation, not
proof that those chip dates are good. Historical points retain their historical scoring rules;
today's eight-chip and FT rules are a counterfactual. We do not invent old defensive-contribution
points. Some missing-player catalogs are one or four events old: ages are recorded and their last
observed prices are **not** claimed to be exact current-deadline prices. This is another reason the
audit cannot grant performance authority. Final receipt:
`data/model/joint_chip_state_replay_v1/results-final.json`.

## Role-change test: why it did not ship

The model compares two recent single-fixture opportunities against up to eight older opportunities
from the same season. Inputs include appearance rates, 60-minute rates, DNP streak and a Bayesian
two-rate evidence feature. Goals/finishing returns do not enter this role signal. A 60-minute event
is explicitly **not** described as a literal start. Blanks/doubles are excluded from training, and
unsupported current fixture counts receive no challenger probability.

Training uses 2023/24 for the 2024/25 test, then 2023/24–2024/25 for the 2025/26 test. A logistic
model with fixed C=0.1 is compared with raw champion probability and a logit-only recalibrator.
The screen requires both Brier and log loss to improve against both controls in both focus folds.

| Decision-focus fold | Champion Brier | Role Brier | Champion log loss | Role log loss |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 2024/25, 8,082 rows | 0.080672 | 0.079477 | 0.294346 | 0.289348 |
| 2025/26, 8,715 rows | 0.083850 | 0.083789 | 0.300581 | 0.300656 |

The latest-season log loss is slightly worse. The screen therefore **fails**; no threshold was
relaxed after seeing it. At GW4 there are only three within-season observations, so the new
non-overlapping regular-role test correctly reports insufficient prior history rather than
pretending to know a tactical change. Historical seasons have been inspected elsewhere in this
project; these are forward folds, not globally untouched research holdouts.

The probability artifact is `data/model/role_shift_research_v1/calibration-v2.json`, bound to its
source/model-code hashes. New receipts freeze all supported current-event role probabilities for
later scoring. No external lineup source was added and no LLM was trained or run.

## Capital allocation and risk

`allocation_research.py` tests £16m/£18m/£20m current bench caps, inclusion of the most expensive
selectable forward and midfielder, and candidates proposed from raw/anchored component means.
They remain legal current actions bounded to two transfers. Proven infeasibility is distinguished
from timeouts; incomplete searches cannot claim a completed comparison.

On the rehearsal snapshot, £16m was unreachable within those transfer bounds. The other six
families were feasible, adding seven distinct plans. Each is scored in all three production
worlds, so a proposal is not judged only by the model that generated it. This is a diagnostic
selection-set reuse, **not an independent holdout**. Component proposals contain only the market
layer already authorized by the production system, not an unvalidated all-bookmaker forecast.

The rehearsal at risk weight 0.25 retained Richarlison → Evanilson even with those extra candidates.
Weights 0 and 0.10 favoured Maguire → Muharemović in the original pool; the raw mean edge
comes with weaker cross-world results. This is sensitivity, not evidence to retune toward whichever
player scored last week. The receipt itemizes current bench cost and when reserves enter later XIs;
simulation scores include autosubs, so scheduled starts alone are not all the value of a reserve.

A frozen GW2/GW3 selector-only replay at weights 0, 0.10, 0.25 and 0.50 produced the same aggregate
**133 net points** for all four settings. It reconstructs legal actions and scores final official
outcomes but does not rebuild historical candidates or future policies. Two previously examined
events cannot establish an optimal risk coefficient. Receipt:
`data/model/frozen_risk_weight_replay_v1/results.json`.
