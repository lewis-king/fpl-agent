Follow the evidence

The answer is not a hunch.

It is the survivor of a sequence of arguments. Every model must earn its place; every squad must survive an objection.

01

Make the past usable.

Before choosing a player, the engine asks whether its memory is trustworthy. Prices, points, minutes and deadlines are rebuilt into one timeline, with the future hidden from every historical prediction.

The normalized feature store contains 244,425 historical rows. Leakage gates assert strictly lagged points and form, deadline-safe snapshots, unique player-event keys and consistent information cutoffs.

Why it matters: a model that can see tomorrow while training will look brilliant and fail on Saturday.

02

Price the fixture, not the badge.

A run of ten conceded goals can be terrible—or merely the cost of facing five elite attacks. The model separates team quality, opponent quality, venue and recency before judging the next match.

An online hierarchical Poisson model updates opponent-adjusted attack and defence, venue splits and promoted-team priors. Five completed seasons provide 1,900 leakage-safe fixture observations.

Deliberate exclusion: official fixture difficulty is not used by the challenger model.

03

Forecast every route to points.

The model does not stop at “good player, easy fixture”. It estimates whether a player appears, how long they play, the goals their team produces and how that becomes goals, assists, clean sheets, contributions and bonus.

559 selectable players are projected over eight horizons. The component layer allocates correlated match events while the xP layer anchors marginal player means to season-forward calibration.

Decision blend: 10% raw component signal, selected on 2024/25 and frozen before the 2025/26 test.

04

Reject clever corrections.

A correction is only useful if it keeps working. The appearance recalibrator helped forwards in both unseen seasons. The same idea failed that standard for goalkeepers, defenders and midfielders—so those changes were refused.

Position-specific logistic recalibration must improve both Brier score and log loss in both strict season-forward slices. Only FWD achieved four wins from four required comparisons.

Explainability is also subtraction: three plausible model changes were prevented from touching the live forecast.

05

Rebuild the new scoring.

The 2026/27 bonus rules change the small edges that decide close calls. Historical bonus allocation was rebuilt exactly; missing tackled events were modelled on older event data and tested forward.

The control reproduces all 380 fixtures and 2,415 bonus points. The RTX 5090 tackled-event proxy reaches 0.892 player-season rank correlation; incomplete goalkeeper save context remains neutral.

Boundary: outfield corrections are promoted. Goalkeeper BPS deltas are not.

06

Search beyond the neighbourhood.

Trying every mathematical team would take longer than football has existed. So the engine combines 542,246 local one- and two-player challengers with 256 distant optimisation restarts and 19,996 legal evolutionary squads. Forty views of value decide which 10,000 deserve simulation.

The combined field contains 562,498 legal squads. Named benchmarks and every global source receive protected places through the 2,000-world screen, exact 512-squad shortlist and 64-squad untouched holdout.

Honest boundary: diversified global challenge—not a false claim to enumerate a 10²⁷-scale roster space.

07

Play the opening month 25,000 times.

A clean sheet disappears for several defenders together. A missed match activates the bench. A captain absence hands the armband to the vice. The simulation preserves those dependencies rather than adding fifteen averages.

Across 1.25 million match scenarios, 3.48 million team goals are allocated to eligible scorers and at most one eligible assister. Legal autosubs, captain fallback, defensive contributions and 3/2/1 bonus are exact.

Football identity check: every team goal reconciles; assist violations are zero.

08

Make the winner survive eight worlds.

The highest average is not automatically the best plan. Squads must cope when goals rise 15%, when the raw football model gets more say, when the safer forecast gets more say, when no transfers are allowed and when GW2 must be banked.

A declared regret objective blends eight base, transfer-policy and scoring-environment scenarios. Candidates more than one point from the best central forecast are ineligible; the winner is therefore robust without abandoning expected points.

Decision: the 4–3–3 wins every leave-one-scenario-out test and naturally rolls GW2.

09

Force the old champion into the final.

C007 is not quietly discarded. It receives the same transfer plans and the same 25,000 match worlds as every finalist. It still owns the highest central average—but falls to twelfth when fragility matters.

The new leader surrenders 0.219 central points to C007, inside the one-point eligibility cap, while cutting robust regret from 1.731 to 0.709. Every protected benchmark survives both elimination cuts.

Why the crown moves: resilience across assumptions, not a noisy 0.2-point headline.

10

Ask if five weeks can see far enough.

An easy August can seduce a picker into building a squad with an expiry date. So every finalist is played through GW8, with distant fixtures discounted, GW6–8 isolated and transfer freedom deliberately removed.

The audit adds 128 horizon-native challengers, 5,000 selection worlds and 25,000 untouched eight-week worlds. A 5–3–2 wins that one audit by 0.697 decayed points, but suffers 4.407 points of regret when goals run 15% higher.

Synthesis: the 4–3–3 remains champion unless uncertain horizon evidence receives more than the live boundary shown in the decision receipt.

11

The new provisional XI.

Bruno takes the armband in a 4–3–3. The squad spends the full £100m, keeps a playable rotation bench and is not materially tied to its first five fixtures. Every name remains conditional on the deadline refresh.

R-dc7de17def ranks first across the joint five/eight-week synthesis. It is only 0.697 decayed points from the eight-week optimum, wins the fixed-squad test and naturally banks the first two transfers in the adaptive GW1–8 policy.

Not a guarantee: this is the current robust champion under frozen data, models, assumptions and seeds.
